    High-Yield Viva Questions & Answers for Examiner Evaluation

    Q1: What was your specific assigned Quality Goal for this course project?

    Answer: My assigned quality goal is Q14 - Improve Navigation. In academic and industrial inventory systems, operators spend up to 40% of their operational time navigating complex menus. Q14 requires five measurable navigation improvements: universal breadcrumbs on every screen, top navigation bar accessible anywhere, major modules reachable within ≤ 2 clicks, a dedicated Home button, and full keyboard shortcut navigation.

    Q2: How is the Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule) demonstrated in your project?

    Answer: We performed empirical defect frequency tracking on 49 usability and inventory friction events. Our live dual-axis Pareto Chart plots defect counts as descending bars and cumulative percentages as an ogive curve. We proved that the top 4 defect modes ("No clear return path", "Module hidden from nav", "Too many clicks", and "Shortcut unavailable") account for 76% of total friction. By fixing these "vital few" root causes through Q14 navigation standards, we eliminated the vast majority of operational delays.

    Q3: Explain how FMEA and RPN were used to minimize project risks.

    Answer: In Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA), each risk is scored across Severity (S: 1-10), Occurrence (O: 1-10), and Detection (D: 1-10). The Risk Priority Number is calculated as RPN = S × O × D. Any failure mode with RPN ≥ 150 is designated "Critical Risk". For example, "Major module hidden from nav" scored S=8, O=5, D=5 (RPN = 200). Our corrective action was implementing a persistent sticky top navigation bar with active indicators, reducing subsequent detection risk significantly.

    Q4: How does ABC Inventory Stratification relate to Total Quality Management?

    Answer: ABC analysis is Pareto's 80/20 law applied to inventory valuation ($Quantity \times Unit Price$). Class A items represent roughly 70% of invested capital and require tight daily quality control, exact cycle counts, and senior management oversight. Class B items represent ~20% of value with weekly standard EOQ replenishment. Class C items account for ~10% of value and use bulk safety replenishment. This prevents tying up working capital on low-turnover items.

    Q5: What is the Six Sigma DMAIC framework and how does this web app follow it?

    Answer: DMAIC stands for: Define (identifying customer requirements through the CTQ tree & SIPOC), Measure (logging defect counts and stock movement events in checksheets), Analyze (Ishikawa 6M fishbone and Pareto 80/20 root cause analysis), Improve (deploying Q14 navigation, responsive web UI, and Wilson's EOQ optimizer), Control (ISO 9001 immutable audit logs, low-stock exception alerts, and Deming PDCA cycles).
